## Build the weather core, then test its coverage

Weather is a major Regulation M-B theme because the official overview names sun, snow, sand, and rain as viable directions. A good type-chart workflow begins with the weather centerpiece, but it should end with the other five slots.

For example, Mega Charizard Y’s Fire/Flying typing is weak to Water, Electric, and Rock in the standard chart, while it resists several types that can make it attractive beside a sun core. That is only the start. A Rock attack is a 4x standard weakness, yet the turn’s target, attack availability, speed, weather, item, and partner can make the board state very different from the chart alone.

Similarly, Mega Swampert’s Water/Ground typing gives it a single standard weakness—Grass—but that weakness is 4x. When adding rain partners, ask whether they punish Grass attackers, can reposition safely, or create the same secondary weakness. The [Water/Ground combination page](/types/water-ground) is useful for that focused check, while the [Grass type guide](/types/grass) shows the attacking side of the interaction.

## Use a six-slot weakness audit

The official overview repeatedly describes pairs and supporting cores rather than isolated Pokémon. That is the right mindset for type planning: do not merely add one answer to each threat; look for repeated liabilities across the whole selection.

1. Add the intended Mega Evolution and weather setter first.
2. Check every member for shared standard weaknesses, especially Ground, Fighting, Rock, Water, and Grass when those types overlap with the core.
3. Mark resistances and immunities, then ask whether those answers can enter the field safely in a Double Battle.
4. Review actual moves, Abilities, weather interactions, and speed control after the chart has revealed the pressure points.

The [team weakness calculator](/pokemon/team-calculator) makes that audit faster by showing repeated weaknesses, resistances, and immunities across six defensive typings. It cannot evaluate every Champions mechanic, but it is a clear way to catch a coverage problem before investing time in a full battle plan.

## What this guide does not claim

Pokémon.com's overview reports the current Regulation M-B landscape and gives official examples of successful ideas. This guide does not turn those examples into guaranteed rankings, exact win rates, mandatory movesets, or a prediction for the next tournament. It also does not assume that a standard type advantage overrides an Ability, weather effect, item, move choice, target selection, or teammate support.

Use the chart to frame the question, validate the live format details in Pokémon Champions, and adjust after real Double Battle experience. That approach keeps the fast, reliable part of type planning separate from the facts that only the actual battle can settle.

## Pokémon Champions Season M-5 FAQ

### When does Pokémon Champions Season M-5 end?

Pokémon's official August 2026 events announcement says Ranked Battles Season M-5 and Battle Pass Season M-5 run until September 8, 2026, at 6:59 p.m. PDT.

### What type matchups should I check for Chandelure, Excadrill, and Scrafty?

For the standard chart, check Chandelure as Fire/Ghost, Excadrill as Ground/Steel, and Scrafty as Dark/Fighting. Scrafty has a 4× Fairy weakness. Those results are a transparent type baseline; actual Champions choices also depend on moves, Abilities, items, teammates, and the battle format.

### What is Pokémon Champions Regulation M-B?

Regulation M-B is the current Pokémon Champions Double Battles regulation discussed in Pokémon.com's July 24, 2026 strategy overview. It introduces new Mega-Evolved Pokémon and features several weather and balance archetypes.

### Which type matchups should I check for Mega Charizard Y teams?

Start with Fire/Flying: Water, Electric, and Rock are super effective in the standard chart, with Rock dealing 4x damage before other battle effects. Then check the actual moves, Abilities, weather, items, and partners in your battle.

### Does a type advantage guarantee a win in Pokémon Champions?

No. Type effectiveness is a useful starting point, but Double Battles also depend on moves, Abilities, weather, speed order, switching, items, targeting, and teammates.
