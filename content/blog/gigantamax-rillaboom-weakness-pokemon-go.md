## The first weakness check: Fire, then role fit

For most players, **Fire** is the first type to check against Gigantamax Rillaboom. Fire is super effective into Grass, many Fire attackers are built for raid-style content, and Fire damage usually translates cleanly when the boss’s defensive type is the main question. But “Fire first” is not the same as “any Fire Pokémon is correct.” In a Max Battle, the team also needs enough staying power, enough Max Move pressure, and enough coordination to avoid wasting limited resources.

The better rule is: start with Fire, then ask whether the Pokémon actually fits the Max Battle job. If your Fire option has poor Max move access, underleveled stats, or a typing that is punished by Rillaboom’s available attacks, a Flying or Ice option may be safer. If a teammate already supplies strong Fire pressure, you may create more value by bringing a bulky support-compatible answer instead of duplicating a fragile attacker.

## Original judgment: how to build a Max Battle team for Rillaboom

Gigantamax Rillaboom asks for a different kind of planning than a normal weakness article. A thin page can say “Grass is weak to Fire, Ice, Poison, Flying, and Bug.” A useful page tells you how to turn that into a team decision under constraints. The main constraint is that Max Battle attempts often punish lopsided teams: four glass cannons may show excellent type pressure on paper and still fail if they cannot absorb enough hits, reach key Max Move windows, or coordinate shields and healing.

Build the team in layers. The first layer is your primary damage line: one or more attackers with super-effective Max pressure, usually Fire or Flying. The second layer is survival: Pokémon that can stay on the field long enough to keep pressure consistent instead of forcing repeated relobbies or resource waste. The third layer is lobby coordination: if everyone brings the same fragile answer, the group can lose flexibility. A balanced group should include at least one player thinking about endurance and one player thinking about clean damage windows.

Timing matters too. If you have limited Max Particles, do not spend them only because a type is technically super effective. Spend when your local group can actually complete the battle, when your chosen attacker has the right Max move upgraded, and when the Power Spot timing gives you enough attempts. If you are missing the ideal Fire attacker, it is often better to bring your best prepared Flying or Ice option than to force an underbuilt Fire pick. Super-effective damage is a multiplier, not a magic eraser for weak preparation.

The biggest exception is move pressure. A Grass-type boss may carry coverage that punishes some obvious answers. If live reports show Rillaboom pressuring Fire or Flying picks unusually well, shift toward the super-effective type that survives the actual move pattern, not the one that sounds best in a vacuum. This is where a type calculator and a live battle report solve different problems: the calculator gives the shortlist, while the live format decides which shortlisted option deserves resources.

## When Poison and Bug are worth considering

Poison and Bug are easy to dismiss because Fire, Flying, and Ice feel more natural against Grass. That dismissal is sometimes correct, but not always. In Pokémon GO, the best practical answer can be the Pokémon you actually have powered up, with the move access the format actually uses. If your Poison or Bug roster has a stronger Max-ready candidate than your Fire roster, it deserves a comparison rather than automatic rejection.

Use Poison when the candidate has good bulk and does not invite a bad coverage matchup. Use Bug when the available Pokémon can keep damage flowing and your group lacks better super-effective pressure. Do not use either type simply for novelty. The goal is not to prove an off-meta point; the goal is to beat a Grass-type Gigantamax boss with the cleanest combination of damage, survival, and resource efficiency.

## Gigantamax Rillaboom FAQ

### What is Gigantamax Rillaboom weak to?

Rillaboom is Grass-type, so Fire, Ice, Poison, Flying, and Bug are the standard super-effective types to check first.

No. Fire is usually the first filter, but the best Pokémon GO pick depends on Max move access, bulk, upgrades, Rillaboom’s live moves, and the rest of your lobby.
